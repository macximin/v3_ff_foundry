This profile covers modern finance, chaebol, IMF, corporate revenge, and legal
predation surfaces. It is not a moral softening layer.

## Commercial Assets

- Money must become objects: promissory notes, bonds, seals, ledgers, contracts,
  registration papers, collateral, shares, deposits, wire receipts.
- Future knowledge should be verified before trust is granted.
- Legal predation is a pleasure asset when payoff, reader indemnity, and visible
  enemy cost are clear.
- Institutions matter: banks, courts, tax offices, holding companies, creditors,
  auditors, newspapers, and market rumor all create pressure.
- Finance exposition should be carried by a room, a document, a call, a signature,
  or a body reaction. Lecture-mode finance is weak.

## Stage Routing

### pitch

Ask whether the protagonist's edge can turn into money, status, or leverage in
the first 5 episodes. Do not turn pitch feedback into prose or voice doctrine.

### story

Check that the Spine sells one repeatable engine and the Anchor Rail binds money,
institutional authority, and human aftershock. Keep distant anchors sparse.

### episode_bet

Require a visible conversion, human aftershock, next pressure, and donor receipt.
A future payday alone is not enough if the episode owes immediate satisfaction.

### manuscript

Look for paper, phone calls, rooms, signatures, sweat, silence, and power
reactions. Money should be readable without a classroom paragraph.

### review

Harvest finance failures as scope signals, not as blanket anti-creativity rules.

## Do Not

- Do not make cold exploitation morally harmless unless the work's payoff needs
  reader indemnity.
- Do not soften the protagonist into a rescuer when the commercial asset is
  legal predation.
- Do not promote one work's prop choice into a universal rule.
- Do not treat familiar chaebol/revenge codes as originality failures.
